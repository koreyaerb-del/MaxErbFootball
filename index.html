LOSS Cumberlands 38 – 21 Bethel
Saturday, Oct. 17, 2026 · Homecoming · Walter Butler Stadium, McKenzie, TN

The Wildcats fell to the University of the Cumberlands (Ky.) in their Homecoming game, dropping to 2–4 overall (0–1 in Mid-South Conference play). Bethel's 21 points were a step up from the shutdowns earlier in the losing stretch, but the Cumberlands offense pulled away.

Quarterback situation

  • Silas Teat (#4) handled the quarterback snaps and appeared to hurt his hand late in the game. He now sits at 51-of-90 passing for 754 yards, 4 TD, 4 INT on the season.
  • Jaylan Brown (#10) — who had been competing with Teat for starts — was hurt and did not play. His status is uncertain. (His season passing line did not change this week, confirming he didn't throw.)
  • With Brown out, Max moved up to QB2 for the game. Even so — and even with Teat's hand injury late — Max did not take a snap.

Direct From MaxMax's Status & Coaches' Feedback

Playing time. Max dressed but did not play in either of the last two games (Faulkner on Oct. 10 and the Cumberlands Homecoming game on Oct. 17). His only appearance on the season remains a single special-teams tackle; he has no passing, rushing, or receiving stats.

In practice, Max has performed well, and the coaches have consistently pointed to his athleticism and ability to make plays.

What the coaches have told Max

The staff's direct feedback: they'd like to see Max read the defense better, but they've acknowledged he has shown improvement, especially given he is new to their system. The core tension is one of style. Max is a gamer — he likes to make plays even when the called play may not be there. The coaches want more of a system quarterback: someone who follows the play call and the read progression. Even though they value his playmaking, they want more control, and they want Max to work through all of his reads first before breaking the pocket.

That expectation runs against Max's instincts, and the mismatch has been a real source of frustration for him.

Bigger-picture concerns

Beyond his own role, Max reports that a number of players on the team have voiced frustration with the coaching staff — particularly with how the system is being run and coached. It's a concern Max shares and has echoed himself. That kind of locker-room unhappiness is not a good sign, and it's worth keeping an eye on. The season as a whole is not going well.

The recruiting promise

When Max was recruited, the staff indicated he would get playing time this season — at minimum, game reps at receiver / athlete, or a run-pass-option "package" designed specifically for him. That was a significant part of why Max chose Bethel over other scholarship offers. So far, none of that has materialized, and Max isn't sure exactly why.

Off the field

For the school year overall, Max likes his teammates and is happy with school — his classes, the community, and his social life. The clear frustration is the lack of game action.

Our TakeFamily Perspective

Based on what we know: even if the coaches weren't ready to trust Max with the full offense, his athleticism and playmaking — things they point to routinely, and which showed up on tape against real competition last year — seem like exactly the kind of tools worth putting on the field for a handful of plays, just to see what he can do. That simply hasn't happened yet.
